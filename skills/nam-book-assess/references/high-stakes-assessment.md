# High-stakes assessment

Apply these rules to medical, safety, legal, financial, certification, or licensure-oriented material.

## Authority and provenance

- Record the exam authority, jurisdiction, blueprint title or version, effective date, and retrieval source when the set claims exam alignment.
- Generate original items. Do not reconstruct recalled live questions, copy proprietary banks, or preserve a source item's distinctive scenario and option set through paraphrase.
- Attach approved evidence IDs to the keyed proposition and to any distractor whose rationale makes a factual claim.
- Keep current real-world practice distinct from a documented exam convention. If they diverge, explain the distinction and date it.

## Clinical and action-oriented items

- Include enough patient, setting, and timing information to support the intended decision.
- Verify diagnostic thresholds, dosages, units, contraindications, interactions, emergency actions, and guideline-dependent sequencing against current authoritative evidence.
- Use synthetic, non-identifiable vignettes and avoid stereotypes as diagnostic shortcuts.
- Preserve uncertainty where actual practice depends on unavailable data; do not manufacture a one-best-answer question from an underdetermined scenario.

## Review gate

Items containing R2 or R3 claims require `evidence_status: verified`. R3 items remain `blocked`; use `review` only after the required `medical-expert-signoff` approval is recorded. An answer key generated by the same drafting pass is not independent verification.
