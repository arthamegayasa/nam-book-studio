## Identity

Nam is an original Bali-starling page spirit: a friendly guide formed from the visual world of books, learning, and Balinese warmth. Nam is inspired by a bird and a page, not presented as a zoologically exact Bali myna or as a sacred or ceremonial figure.

## Fixed visual anchors

- Upright white page-like bird body with a soft, simple silhouette.
- Lightly irregular charcoal or black hand-drawn contour.
- A small swept crest that reads as feathers and turned book pages.
- Small round charcoal eyes with tiny white catchlights; the expression is warm, attentive, and friendly.
- No blue eye patches, blue facial rings, or other blue facial markings.
- Compact charcoal diamond beak, dark wing-hands, and orange or terracotta legs and feet.
- A dark charcoal book commonly carried under one wing; an ochre or marigold open book may become the active teaching object.
- A narrow orange geometric sash at the lower body. Treat it as an original design accent, not as a named traditional textile unless its specific source and permission have been verified.

Working palette roles are ink black, soft charcoal, page white, marigold or ochre, and terracotta orange. Sample production values from the approved assets and store them in the character-bible artifact rather than guessing new values per image.

## Character and action

Nam is curious, patient, quietly capable, and lightly playful. Nam helps the reader inspect an idea rather than performing for attention.

Prefer actions that carry the learning job: reading, annotating, comparing, sorting, pointing, measuring, assembling, opening a path, holding a model steady, or guiding another figure through a decision. If removing Nam leaves the illustration's teaching action unchanged, revise the pose or omit the character.

Keep emotion legible through posture, gaze, and small gestures. Preserve the round eyes and restrained expression; exaggerated sticker reactions, sparkling anime eyes, or baby proportions break continuity.

## Cultural respect

- Use Balinese references with a named purpose, source, and review status.
- Prefer original geometry, color rhythm, landscape cues, everyday craft materials, and locally grounded context over a collage of recognizable symbols.
- Keep sacred scripts, offerings, temple objects, ritual dress, deities, and ceremonial gestures out of decorative or comic roles. Include them only when the subject requires them, authoritative sources support the depiction, and an appropriate cultural reviewer approves it.
- Do not label an invented pattern as batik, songket, endek, poleng, or another specific tradition without evidence that the design and use are accurate.
- Avoid presenting Bali as timeless, exotic, uniformly spiritual, or interchangeable with other Indonesian cultures.
- For conservation or zoological claims about the Bali myna, use the research ledger. The mascot design is not evidence about the species.

## Originality boundary

Nam's silhouette, face, books, palette, actions, and cultural direction must remain distinct from Xiaohei, Tony, other public skill mascots, publisher characters, and supplied third-party artwork. Use high-level educational illustration principles without importing another mascot's body shape, signature props, example composition, name, or calibration image.

Do not claim that generation proves trademark availability, exclusive ownership, or freedom from all third-party rights. Record provenance and obtain separate legal review if the project needs brand clearance.
