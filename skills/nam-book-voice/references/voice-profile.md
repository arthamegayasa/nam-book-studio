# Voice profile

A voice profile records repeatable editorial decisions. It is not a bag of catchphrases or a mandate to make every paragraph sound identical.

## Evidence base

Use representative, authorized samples across more than one topic when possible. Record the source, genre, audience, date, approximate length, and whether it reflects the author's current preferred voice. Separate:

- **Observed:** a tendency supported by samples.
- **Chosen:** a direction the author explicitly wants.
- **Avoid:** a tendency the author rejects or a boundary imposed by the project.
- **Unknown:** an aspect for which the evidence is insufficient.

Do not retain long sample passages in the profile. Short examples should demonstrate a decision without becoming reusable copied prose.

## Profile fields

- author or house-voice identifier and version
- authorization and sample provenance
- intended reader relationship
- stance, warmth, confidence, and degree of direct address
- sentence and paragraph cadence ranges
- diction, jargon introduction, and technical density
- explanation habits and preferred evidence framing
- transitions, signposting, questions, and rhetorical emphasis
- humor and metaphor boundaries
- treatment of uncertainty, disagreement, warnings, and calls to action
- locale-specific choices, including form of address
- characteristic strengths to preserve
- repetitive habits, filler, and signatures to avoid
- confidence by field and unresolved author decisions

## Apply and audit

Apply the profile as constraints and ranges, not a phrase generator. Preserve natural variation by section purpose: a warning, worked example, narrative opening, and technical definition should not share identical cadence.

Audit representative passages across chapters. Cite block IDs for drift findings and classify them as structural, diction, cadence, stance, technical density, or locale drift. Keep voice findings separate from factual or pedagogical findings and route those to their owning skills.

For a third-party reference voice, convert the request into high-level traits such as concise, conversational, restrained, image-rich, or formally analytical. Produce new phrasing and avoid signature imitation.
